Serialize process signal registration and restoration across domains

**Depends on:** None.

**Premise verified (2026-09-28):** Traced `framework/ocaml/sol-runtime/lib/sol_runtime.ml:25-39` with callers and existing tests at origin/main `2dfa5694`. Evidence and reproduction limits are recorded in the source audit.

## Problem

The atomic registration list is updated separately from checking emptiness, installing/restoring signal handlers and saving the mutable previous dispositions. Concurrent domains can overwrite the original disposition with Sol's own handler, leaving it installed after all registrations close. With an empty listener list the leaked handler swallows the first SIGTERM.

## Remediation

Serialize registration/removal and the associated saved-disposition bookkeeping with one shared synchronization boundary. Keep the signal callback lock-free. Preserve the existing first-signal graceful stop and second-signal termination behavior.

## Acceptance criteria

- A child-process regression concurrently installs and releases two runtimes and confirms original SIGTERM/SIGINT dispositions are restored. Retain single-runtime controls and verify registered stop promises resolve. Record the TypeScript verdict: this fixes OCaml process-global bookkeeping without adding a new application contract.
- Record the application demo and per-language verdict in completion notes wherever the change affects an application contract.

## Completion (2026-09-29)

- **Premise re-verified** at origin/main `788e688d`: `register` read `Atomic.get registered`, installed the handlers and saved `previous`, then appended under a separate CAS; `unregister` removed and restored under a separate check. Two domains could both observe an empty list, both install the handler, and the second saved Sol's *own* handler as the "previous" disposition — so the last restore left it installed.
- **Fix.** A stdlib `Mutex` (`registration_mutex` via `with_registration_lock`) now serializes the emptiness check, the install/save, the atomic-list update, the removal, and the restore. `handle` is untouched and remains lock-free: it only reads the `registered` atomic and writes the pipe. The first-signal graceful stop and second-signal re-raise behaviour are unchanged, and the new lock is internal (`sol_runtime.mli` is unchanged).
- **Tests** (`framework/ocaml/sol-runtime/test/test_signal.ml`): a new `--concurrent-registration-child` mode runs up to 2000 rounds of two domains that barrier-synchronize, each installing an `install_signal_handler` in its own switch and then releasing it; after both switches close it checks `Sys.signal Sys.sigterm Sys.Signal_default` and exits 1 on a leak. `test_concurrent_registration_restores_disposition` runs it as a **child process** and requires exit 0. **Mutation-checked**: replacing the lock with a direct call makes the child exit 1 on all three runs while the fixed binary exits 0 on all three. The single-runtime controls are retained unchanged (`SIGTERM`/`SIGINT` resolve the stop promise, one signal reaches every registration, disposition restored after one switch, second signal terminates) — all 6 tests pass.
- Validation: full `dune build`; `dune fmt --preview` clean; no-comments policy holds.
- **Application demo: not applicable** — process-global signal bookkeeping with no app-author surface. **TypeScript verdict: not applicable** — this corrects OCaml process-global state and adds no new framework/application contract; the TypeScript framework has no shared signal-registration registry to mirror.

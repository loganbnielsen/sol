title: Dependent ticket work stacks instead of idling on review
---

**Depends on:** None.

## Decision

When ticket B's work depends on ticket A's, B starts immediately: it branches from A's branch, opens
its PR against A's branch, and proceeds while A is in review. A dependent PR never merges before its
prerequisite, so when A lands, B is retargeted to `main` and A's final state is reconciled into B
before B lands.

Stacking is **not** allowed when the dependency is unresolved — A still carries `## Open Questions`,
`## Decision Required` or `## Blocked On` — or when B's correct implementation depends on the outcome
of A's review. In those cases the boundary is a decision, not a merge, and B waits for it.

Ownership, validation and the one-ticket/one-PR rule are unchanged: each stacked ticket keeps its own
worktree, its own branch and its own PR, and the ticket-move guard still holds each branch to moving
its own ticket.

## Rationale

Waiting for CI and review before starting dependent work serialises a queue that does not need to be
serial: the next ticket usually needs the *interface* the previous one establishes, which is already
visible in its branch, not the merge itself. The cost is a reconciliation step when the parent
changes, which is smaller than the idle time and is bounded by the requirement to reconcile before
landing.

## Consequences

- Written up in `CONTRIBUTING.md` § *Isolation and ownership* (the mechanics) with the rule and its
  limits stated in `AGENTS.md`; those two are the whole statement, and neither restates the other.
- `soldev pipeline merge` refuses a PR whose base branch has no required checks configured, so a
  stacked PR must be retargeted to `main` after its prerequisite merges. If stacked work becomes
  common, the tool could accept a base that is another ticket's merged branch, but the retarget is
  correct today and needs no tooling change.
- The cold-start dependency gate still reports B as blocked while A is not in `DONE/` — `/work`
  refuses to create a worktree for a ticket whose dependencies are unmet, and `soldev pipeline check`
  and `pipeline merge` say so too. That is correct and is not a contradiction: the gate governs
  merging and cold starts, not stacked execution. A stacked ticket is started by branching from its
  prerequisite's branch with that branch named explicitly, and its merge waits for A to land
  regardless of how the work started.
